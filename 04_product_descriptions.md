# Product description framework

Most arbitrage stores fail here. They paste the AliExpress description verbatim — full of broken English, "high quality material," "100% brand new," and zero benefits. That's why they don't convert.

Below is a tight framework + 3 worked examples covering the most common arbitrage categories. Use the framework, fill in your specifics, and you'll outperform 95% of competing arbitrage stores.

---

## The framework: HOOK · PAS · BULLETS · SPECS · GUARANTEE

```
[HOOK — one short bold sentence that names the customer's
desire or pain. Imagine they're scrolling TikTok and just
landed here. You have 2 seconds.]

[PAS paragraph — 2–4 sentences:
  Problem: what frustrates the customer
  Agitation: why current options fall short
  Solution: how this product fixes it]

What you'll love:
  • [Benefit 1 — not a feature. Translate "stainless steel"
    to "won't rust, lasts years, looks good on the counter"]
  • [Benefit 2]
  • [Benefit 3]
  • [Benefit 4 — optional]

Specs:
  • Materials:
  • Dimensions:
  • Weight:
  • What's in the box:
  • Care:

Guarantee:
30-day returns. Free US shipping over $50. Real-human support.
```

```
Guarantee:
Try it for 30 days. If you don't love it, send it back for a full
refund. Free US shipping on orders over $50.
```

## Editing your existing descriptions — checklist

For each product on your store right now, audit:

- [ ] **First line is a hook**, not a feature list
- [ ] **No phrases like** "high quality," "100% brand new," "professional grade," "premium material" — these mean nothing and read as spam
- [ ] **No machine-translated grammar** — read aloud. If it sounds like a robot, rewrite
- [ ] **Benefits, not features** — every spec should answer "why does the customer care?"
- [ ] **Specs are specific** — actual dimensions, materials, what's in the box
- [ ] **Guarantee block** — repeat the return policy on every product page
- [ ] **At least 3 product images** — multiple angles + a lifestyle shot if possible

## Quick image audit

The screenshot you sent shows what look like default Shopify t-shirt placeholder images. If those are your actual product photos, you need real ones — placeholders are an instant trust killer. Three options ranked by ease:

1. **Pull supplier images** if your supplier (AliExpress, Spocket, etc.) has decent photos — most do. Shopify apps like DSers will import them automatically.
2. **Photograph items yourself** if you have inventory. Phone camera, near a window, white sheet as backdrop. Do this for hero shots — they outperform stock.
3. **Generate lifestyle scenes** with Canva or a free AI image tool to give context (e.g., the desk light on a styled desk).

Aim for **4–5 images per product**: 1 clean white-bg shot, 2 detail/angle shots, 1 lifestyle/in-use, 1 size reference.
